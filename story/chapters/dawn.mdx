Launched in September 2007, the Dawn mission was the first spacecraft to orbit two separate extraterrestrial destinations beyond the Earth-Moon system. Enabled by high-efficiency ion propulsion that accelerated xenon gas to 89,000 miles per hour, Dawn traveled deep into the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

At Vesta, Dawn discovered a massive south-pole impact basin called Rheasilvia with a central mountain towering twice the height of Mount Everest. At dwarf planet Ceres, it revealed an active, icy world with ancient subsurface brine reservoirs and cryovolcanic domes. When its reaction control thrusters ran out of hydrazine in late 2018, mission controllers deliberately placed the silent satellite into a high, stable orbit that will remain stable for decades, ensuring Earth microbes never crash into Ceres's potentially habitable subsurface ice.
