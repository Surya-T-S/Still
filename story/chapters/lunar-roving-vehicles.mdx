Before the arrival of the Lunar Roving Vehicle on Apollo 15, astronauts were restricted to exploring on foot within a strict radius of their landers—limited by spacesuit cooling water, oxygen supplies, and the physical exhaustion of hopping in one-sixth gravity.

Built by Boeing and General Motors, each 460-pound rover used four quarter-horsepower electric hub motors powered by two 36-volt non-rechargeable silver-zinc batteries. Over Apollo 15, 16, and 17, the three buggies covered more than 56 cumulative miles of lunar terrain, climbing boulder-strewn slopes and hauling hundreds of pounds of core samples. When each mission ended, the astronauts parked the rovers roughly 300 feet away, aligning their high-gain antennas to film the lift-off for mission control.
