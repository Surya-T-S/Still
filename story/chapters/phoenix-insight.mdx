Phoenix and InSight were stationary landers engineered by NASA's Discovery Program to probe Mars's hidden interior and polar chemistry. Landing in Vastitas Borealis in May 2008, Phoenix utilized a robotic trenching arm to dig into arctic permafrost, analyzing soil chemistry in onboard mass spectrometers and directly confirming water ice just inches below the red soil.

In November 2018, InSight touched down on the smooth volcanic plains of Elysium Planitia, deploying the SEIS seismometer under a wind-and-thermal shield. Over four years, InSight registered 1,319 marsquakes, including meteorite impact vibrations, mapping the thickness of the Martian crust and proving Mars has a large, molten liquid core. With no cleaning winds to clear its circular solar panels, InSight transmitted its final reading on December 15, 2022, as winter dust starved its electronics.
