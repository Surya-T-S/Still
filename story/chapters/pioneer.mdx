Pioneer 10 and 11 were the pathfinders of outer solar system exploration. Launched on Atlas-Centaur rockets in 1972 and 1973, they were the first spacecraft to navigate the main asteroid belt, photograph Jupiter's Great Red Spot up close, and explore Saturn's outer rings—paving the navigational corridor for the subsequent Voyager missions.

Bolted to the antenna supports of both spacecraft is a 6-by-9-inch gold-anodized aluminum plaque designed by Carl Sagan and Frank Drake. Engraved with diagrams of the hydrogen atom, human figures, and a radial pulsar map calculating Earth's exact cosmic coordinates, the plaques were intended as a message for any civilization that might intercept the derelict craft. While their radio transmitters fell silent over twenty years ago, Pioneer 10 and 11 will drift through the interstellar vacuum for hundreds of millions of years.
