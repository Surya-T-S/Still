The Mars Exploration Rovers landed in January 2004 equipped with microscopic imagers, rock abrasion tools, and Mössbauer spectrometers to search for geological clues of past liquid water. Engineered for three months of operations, both rovers benefited from unexpected "cleaning events," where passing dust devils swept grit off their glass solar panels.

At Gusev Crater, Spirit's locked wheel unintentionally trenched the Martian topsoil, exposing bright deposits of 90% pure silica—the signature of ancient volcanic hydrothermal vents. On the other side of the planet, Opportunity discovered hematite spherules nicknamed "blueberries," proving they formed inside mineral-rich standing water. Spirit became embedded in a soft sand trap in 2009 and froze during the southern winter. Opportunity persevered until June 10, 2018, when an unprecedented global dust storm blocked 99% of sunlight, permanently silencing the veteran explorer in Perseverance Valley.
