Viking 1 touched down in Chryse Planitia on July 20, 1976, marking the first successful American landing on the Martian surface. Together with Viking 2 in Utopia Planitia, the twin orbiters and landers conducted the first comprehensive in-situ exploration of Mars.

Powered by dual radioisotope thermoelectric generators, Viking 1 operated for 2,245 sols (more than six Earth years), far exceeding its 90-day design baseline. It returned over 50,000 photos, gathered continuous meteorological records of winds and atmospheric pressures, and used a three-meter mechanical boom to deliver soil samples into miniature biological test chambers. On November 11, 1982, an unintended uplink command from JPL inadvertently broke communications with the lander. In January 1982, NASA officially named Viking 1 the Thomas Mutch Memorial Station in honor of the mission's chief imaging scientist.
